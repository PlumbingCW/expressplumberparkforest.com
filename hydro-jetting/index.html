A line that keeps backing up and will not clear

While you wait for the jetting crew

Repeated backups are a warning that something is coating or narrowing the pipe. Keep the household from adding to it.

  • Hold off on laundry, dishwasher cycles and long showers.
  • Flush only when necessary if the toilets still work, and stop if water rises elsewhere.
  • Do not pour more chemical cleaner into the line, since the crew may have to work around it.
  • Note which fixtures slow down first, because that helps us find the starting point.
  • Mention any past cable visits, roots or camera findings when you call.

What hydro jetting actually is

Hydro jetting uses a hose, a pump and plain water. The pump pushes water at very high pressure, far above anything from a garden hose, through a nozzle at the end of the hose. Jets on the nozzle face backward to pull the hose forward through the pipe and others spray sideways to scour the wall.

A cable pokes a hole through a clog. A jet cleans the inside surface, the way a pressure washer cleans a deck instead of poking at one stain. That difference is why jetting is the better choice for packed grease and sludge.

The pressure and nozzle choice

Different nozzles do different jobs. A penetrating nozzle drives forward through a blockage, a rotating nozzle cuts a wide scrubbing pattern and a chain or blade style head can cut roots. The operator picks one based on pipe material, pipe size and what the camera or cable showed first.

Pressure is a setting, not a fixed number. A small kitchen branch gets a gentler setting than a thick main line, and an old pipe gets less than a modern PVC drain. We would rather clean a line a little more slowly than push a fragile pipe past what it can take.

When snaking has already failed

If the same line clogs every month or two, something is coating the pipe, not just plugging it. Restaurants and busy kitchens know this well, and homes with heavy cooking, big families or years of grease down the sink see it too. A jet at the right setting clears the lining so the pipe returns to a closer match of its original diameter.

Roots behave the same way. Hair-thin roots enter at joints and catch paper and wipes. Cut once with a cable, they grow back, so the stoppage keeps coming. Jetting washes out the debris they trapped, and we will tell you if a sewer line repair at the root entry point is the longer-term fix.

Honest limits and the camera first rule

Hydro jetting is not for every pipe. A collapsed section, a badly cracked line or a joint that has slipped out of alignment can be damaged more by strong water, and a jet can push debris into a break where it fouls the soil around the pipe. We cannot judge that from the surface.

So we put a camera down the line before we jet when the pipe is old, when there is a history of repairs or when the cable hit something solid. A sewer inspection shows what we are working with, and if the picture is bad, we tell you to repair first and clean second.

Cast iron and clay need extra caution

Many older homes have cast iron inside and clay tile in the yard. Cast iron develops rough scale and rust nodules that jets can break free in large flakes. Clay tile has joints every few feet that root easily and can crack from age.

On these pipes we pick a more careful nozzle and a lower setting, watch the return of debris and stop if the camera or the flow suggests a problem. If the line cannot take it, we say so and talk through options instead of forcing the job.

What jetting looks like at your home

Find the entry point

We use a cleanout when there is one. Without a cleanout, access may come through a toilet, a roof vent or a trap, which takes longer.

Check the line

A cable or camera pass shows the first bends, any hard obstruction and the pipe material.

Jet and flush

The nozzle works back and forth while water carries loosened material away. We keep going until the water returns clear and flows freely.

Review the result

We describe what came out, how the pipe looks and how soon you might need a repeat if grease habits stay the same.

A Park Forest reminder about fats and wipes

The Village of Park Forest posts Metropolitan Water Reclamation District guidance that fats, oils, wipes and paper towels should stay out of drains and toilets. That matters for jetting because those materials are what rebuild the coating we just removed, so a clean line stays clean much longer when the household keeps them out.

Same-day and after-hours jetting

Jet equipment is bulkier than a cable machine, so we plan the visit rather than send it blind. Call any hour and we will sort out whether the situation calls for a jet now or a cable first, and we book same-day when our schedule allows.

What affects the cost of hydro jetting

Pipe length and size

A short kitchen line costs less to jet than a long main with a larger diameter.

What is inside the pipe

Light grease takes a pass or two. Thick scale, packed roots or a long neglected line needs more time and more passes.

Access

A cleanout lets the hose go in directly. Pulling a toilet or working from a roof vent adds time.

Camera inspection

A camera pass before or after the jetting is a separate step, but it protects the pipe and documents the result.

Time and urgency

Night, weekend and same-day calls change the schedule and the price compared with a planned weekday visit.
