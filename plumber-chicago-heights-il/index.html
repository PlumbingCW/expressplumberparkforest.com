Corridors, shops and the industrial side

Dixie Highway (IL 1) and Lincoln Highway (US 30) cross here, and the Ford stamping plant has stood on Lincoln Highway since the 1950s. Shops and light industrial buildings along those roads carry large floor drains, mop sinks and rooftop-fed storm lines. Call about commercial plumbing before a tenant moves in, since old drains may not match the new use.

We cannot say what any one building holds, but heavy-use drains clog with grease, grit and debris faster than home lines do. A scheduled jetting visit is often cheaper than an emergency one.

Old downtown blocks and the age of the pipes

The original Carnegie Library opened in 1903 at 1627 Halsted Street, a reminder that parts of the city date to the early 1900s. Buildings from that era may still have cast iron drains and old soldered supply lines. If you rent or own on a block like that, ask for the build year and have a plumber look at the pipe before a remodel.

Drains and sewers that fail first

Chicago Heights grew as an industrial suburb, and older buildings often share one thing, aging underground pipe. We cannot say how old your home is without seeing the deed, so check the build year. If it predates the 1960s, expect cast iron, clay or galvanized steel somewhere in the system.

Cast iron drains scale inside and narrow over decades, which is why a house can have slow drains in several fixtures. A hydro jetting pass clears that buildup better than a cable alone, though we camera first to make sure the pipe can take it.

Before We Arrive in Chicago Heights

Shut the main first

Whether the building is an old two-flat or a shop near the rail lines, find the main valve and close it. Basements here often hide it behind stored boxes, so clear a path to it.

Turn off circuits by the water

Standing water near a fuse box is dangerous. Cut the breakers for the wet area from a dry position, and wait outside if you cannot reach them safely.

Get belongings out of the splash zone

Move paperwork, tools and stock off the floor and away from the drip. Business owners should also photograph the damage for their records.
