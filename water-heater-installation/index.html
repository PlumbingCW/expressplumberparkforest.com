Putting in a water heater is mostly planning. The tank is the easy part, and the location, the fuel supply, the vent path and the drain are what decide whether the job passes inspection and lasts. If you are doing a remodel, finishing a basement or moving the laundry, this is the moment to sort it out.

Where the heater goes and why it matters

A heater needs clearance for service, a floor that can hold a full tank, and a way to get water out if it leaks. Most are in a basement, garage or utility closet. A closet with a gas unit also needs combustion air, and an attic placement needs a drain pan piped to a safe discharge.

Moving a heater farther from the bathrooms means longer hot water runs, so more waiting and more wasted water at the tap. Moving it closer to the fixtures it serves usually pays off. We measure the run and talk through the tradeoff before anyone cuts a pipe.

Conversions between fuels

Converting from electric to gas, or gas to electric, is more than a swap. Going to gas needs a gas line, a shutoff and a vent. Going to electric needs a circuit and breaker, and the old flue should be capped or removed properly.

We look at the cost of running each fuel in your home against the one-time cost of the conversion. Sometimes staying with the current fuel is the better call, and we say that when it is true.

Venting, drain pan and expansion tank

Venting

A gas heater must vent exhaust outdoors with the right pipe, pitch and clearances. A bad vent is a safety hazard, not a cosmetic issue.

Drain pan

A pan with a drain line catches leaks from a tank that will eventually fail. It is especially useful above finished space.

Expansion tank

In a closed system, heated water has nowhere to expand. A small tank on the cold line takes up the pressure.

Relief valve piping

The relief valve must discharge through a pipe to a safe spot, never capped and never routed upward.

Code, permits and the Village of Park Forest

Park Forest adopts the Illinois State Plumbing Code, which is Section 890 of the state administrative code. The Village Building Department at 350 Victory Drive lists water heater replacement as a permit item, and all contractors working in the Village must be Park Forest licensed. Homeowners can verify state plumbing licenses through the Illinois Department of Public Health lookup.

A permit is required for hired and do-it-yourself work alike. Homeowners working alone submit a waiver form, and when a contractor pulls the permit the homeowner signs the proposal or application. Permits are not taken by email or online, so plan time for a drop-off or mail.

Safety steps before a heater is installed

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911. Keep the area around the old heater clear so the plumber can reach the shutoff valves.

Turn off the breaker or gas control only when told to, and never drain a hot tank. Hot water scalds quickly, so keep children and pets away from the work area.
