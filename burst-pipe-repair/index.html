A Pipe Has Burst and Water Is Pouring

Close the main shutoff first. It is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house, and it turns clockwise to close. Then open the lowest faucet and a cold tap upstairs to drain what is left in the pipes.

  • Leave the water heater alone until the lines are refilled, so the elements or burner never run on an empty tank
  • Stay out of any room where water is near outlets, panels or ceiling lights
  • Lift small valuables, rugs and cardboard off the floor
  • Do not use a wet-dry vacuum if the water has reached electrical equipment

Shutoff Steps That Stop the Flood

Know which valve to close

The main shutoff sits where the water line enters the building, often in a basement, utility room or crawlspace. A quarter-turn ball valve closes with a quarter turn, and an older gate valve needs several full turns clockwise.

Try the fixture stop next

If the burst is at a sink or toilet supply line, the small oval stop valve under the fixture can isolate it. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Drain the system

With the main closed, open faucets from the top floor down so the water drains out and the pressure drops. This slows leakage from the break.

If the main valve will not turn, call us right away rather than forcing it, since an old stem can snap. We can replace a failed shutoff as part of the repair.

Electrical Safety Comes Before Cleanup

Water and electricity do not mix, and a burst line often soaks outlets, ceiling fixtures and the panel. If water is in contact with any of those, do not stand in the pool to reach a breaker. Shut off power to the affected area from a dry spot, or leave the house and call us and your electric utility.

A wet panel, sparking outlets or a buzzing sound means leave the building and call 911. We do not work around live wet equipment, so a licensed electrician may need to clear the area before we begin.

What to Record Before You Mop

A clear record helps whatever happens next with your claim. Photograph the broken pipe, the water line on the walls, damaged contents and the meter reading. Keep the failed pipe section if you can, since it shows how the break happened.

Note the time you found the leak and when you closed the valve. We cannot say what your policy covers, so ask your insurer what they need. Our invoice describes the work in plain terms for your file.

Frozen Pipes and Why They Burst

Park Forest winters bring freeze-thaw cycles, and water expands as it freezes. A pipe usually does not split at the ice itself. It splits between the ice blockage and a closed faucet, where trapped water has nowhere to go and pressure builds. The damage often only shows up when the ice melts and water starts to flow.

If a faucet runs a trickle or not at all in freezing weather, a section may be frozen. Open the tap, shut the main, and warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer or heating pad starting nearest the faucet. Never use an open flame or a torch. If you see a bulge or a crack, stop and call us.

Prevention matters most before freeze-thaw weather arrives. Disconnect hoses, insulate pipes in garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls, seal drafts around pipe penetrations, and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. If you plan to leave the house, keep the heat on.

Repairing a Cracked or Broken Pipe

Repair starts with isolating the section and cutting back to clean, sound pipe. A split rarely stays one split, so we check beyond the visible damage for hairline cracks or weakened walls. New pipe, fittings and, where it fits, a union or shutoff go in so future work is easier.

Then we pressure-test the line, restore water slowly, and check the joints with the faucets open to push out air. If walls or ceilings are wet, we point out what needs drying, but drywall and flooring repair is a separate trade. On a same-day visit we stabilize the line first, and after-hours calls get the same pressure test as daytime ones.

Copper Pipe Repair

Copper handles pressure well, but it can pit from the inside, split when frozen, or fail at old solder joints. Repair means cutting out the damaged length and joining new copper with soldered joints or approved press fittings. Corroded copper in several spots usually means the whole run is near the end of its life.

Drain Pipe Repair

Drain pipes break differently from supply lines. They carry water by gravity, so a crack leaks when flow passes through, often as a stain below a bathroom or a damp spot by a cleanout. Cast iron cracks and rusts through, while PVC separates at joints. Repair means cutting out the failed section and replacing it at the right slope. For buried breaks, a camera inspection and sewer line repair may be needed.

Pipe Leak Repair Short of a Full Burst

Not every failure is dramatic. A weeping joint or pinhole can run for months before anyone notices. If you cannot see where the water comes from, our leak detection service can trace it, and a small repair now beats a burst later.

Permits and Licensing in Park Forest

The Village requires every contractor working in Park Forest to be Park Forest licensed, and a permit is needed when you do the work yourself or hire someone. A homeowner doing their own work submits a waiver form, and if a contractor pulls the permit, the homeowner signs the proposal or application. Illinois also requires plumbers to be state licensed, and you can check that on the IDPH license verification tool.

What affects the cost of burst pipe repair

Where the break is

A pipe in an open basement ceiling is quick to reach, while one inside a wall or slab takes more work.

Pipe material

Copper, PEX, CPVC, galvanized and cast iron each need different fittings, tools and labor.

Extent of the damage

One split section is a different job from several failing runs, which may point to partial repiping.

Access and finish work

Cutting into drywall, tile or cabinets adds time, and rebuilding those surfaces is a separate trade.

Time of day

After-hours calls take a technician away from a regular schedule, which can affect the price.
