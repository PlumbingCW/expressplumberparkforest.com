Sewer Inspection in Park Forest, IL

A camera sent down your sewer line shows what is inside the pipe, so repairs and cleaning are based on what we can see instead of guesses.

Before anyone digs up a yard or replaces a pipe, someone should look inside it. A sewer inspection does that with a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable, and it turns a hidden pipe into something you can see on a screen. We schedule these visits by phone, and we can often fit them in on the same day when the calendar allows.

The equipment behind the picture

A push camera has a lit lens mounted on a stiff but flexible rod that feeds into the line from a cleanout or a fixture opening. A monitor shows the live picture. Smaller heads fit three and four inch house drains, and larger ones suit main sewer lines.

A locator adds the second half of the story. The camera head transmits a signal, and a handheld receiver above ground finds it and shows how deep the pipe is. That means we can mark where a problem sits in your yard before a shovel goes in.

A camera also cannot clear a blockage, and a pipe full of sludge can hide its own damage. We sometimes need to cable or jet the line first so the lens can see the wall.

Good times to schedule an inspection

Buying a home

An older sewer lateral can hide cracks or roots that a basic home inspection misses. Looking before closing shows what repairs may be waiting.

After repeated clogs

If cabling gives you only a few weeks of relief, a camera shows whether grease, roots or a damaged section is behind it.

Before digging or landscaping

Knowing where the line runs and how deep it sits keeps trees, patios and new fences away from it.

After a backup

Once water stops rising, the camera tells you whether the cause was a one-time object or a failing pipe.

Foul smells or soggy ground

Odors in the yard or wet spots over the sewer path suggest a leak that a look inside the pipe can confirm.

Buying an older Park Forest home

Park Forest began as a planned community after World War II, and many of its roughly 9,700 housing units date from the decades that followed. Check the build year of the house you are considering, because older homes are more likely to have cast iron, clay or early plastic pipe. A camera look before you buy can save a surprise repair bill after you move in.

How a visit goes

Choose the entry point

We use the cleanout if the house has one, or we remove a toilet or open a trap if not.

Run the camera

The camera moves slowly toward the street while we watch the screen and note anything unusual.

Locate problems

When we find damage, roots or a belly, the locator marks the spot on the ground and shows the depth.

Talk through the findings

We explain what we saw, what needs attention now and what can wait.

Recordings and what to ask for

A recording is useful if you plan to hire another contractor, file an insurance claim or negotiate a home purchase. Ask us about it when you call so we plan for it before we start. We describe each finding by distance from the cleanout, which makes it easier to compare later.

If you are buying and the seller has a recent inspection, ask for it. We can review it with you, though a fresh look is still the most reliable way to see the pipe as it is today.

What happens next

Many inspections end with a clean pipe and a note to keep watching it. Others show a problem, and we explain the options, which may include sewer line repair or regular cleaning. We do not push work the camera does not support.

If the camera shows a blocked line that is backing up right now, we move to sewer line cleaning as the urgent step. That service is available any hour, 24/7, and the inspection can follow once water is draining.

A Park Forest water and flooding note

The Village of Park Forest says urban flooding can cause sewage backups in basements and is replacing wastewater lines. A camera inspection of your private lateral tells you if your side is sound. It does not replace anything the Village does on the street, but it helps you separate your pipe from theirs.

What affects the cost of a sewer inspection

Access

A cleanout lets the camera in quickly. Without one, removing a toilet or opening a trap adds time.

Length of the line

A short lateral costs less to inspect than a long run to the street.

Pipe condition

If debris blocks the view, cleaning before the camera adds a step.

Locating and marking

Finding depth and marking the surface above a problem is extra work beyond a basic run.

Recording and timing

Saving a recording, and evening or weekend scheduling, can change the total.
