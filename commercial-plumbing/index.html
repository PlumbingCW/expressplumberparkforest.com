Your Business Has a Leak or Backup Right Now

Restaurants, Retail Stores and Offices

Each type of business stresses plumbing in its own way. Restaurants push grease, food solids and heavy water use through kitchen lines. Retail stores often have a few restrooms that serve a lot of people. Offices have lower use but tend to have older supply lines, hidden branches and shared risers.

Many Park Forest storefronts sit in multi-tenant buildings. Downtown Park Forest on Main Street, for example, includes a multi-tenant retail building at 200-298 Main St, and in a building like that a drain or riser can serve several suites. That means a backup in one unit may start in another, so it helps to tell the property manager as soon as you call.

After-Hours Leaks, Backups and Water Damage

Overnight and weekend failures are the most expensive because nobody is there to notice them. A burst supply line can run for hours, and a slow drain can back up into a kitchen before the first shift arrives. Our after-hours service starts with stopping the damage, then moves to repair.

For a burst line, see our burst pipe repair page. For drains, we use cabling or hydro jetting as needed, and for repeat blockages we run a camera inspection to see what is happening in the line. If the sewer is the issue, sewer line cleaning and repair may follow.

Grease Trap and Interceptor Cleaning

A grease trap holds fats, oils and food solids before they reach the sewer. When it fills, the grease passes through and hardens in the line, which causes backups and odors. A common rule of thumb is to clean the trap when grease and solids reach about one quarter of its depth.

We pump out the trap, scrape the baffles, check the inlet and outlet for damage, and dispose of the waste properly. The Village flooding guidance, based on MWRD advice, asks residents not to flush or pour fats, oils, wipes or paper towels. That applies to kitchens, too, and it is why a regular cleaning schedule matters. Ask what schedule suits your volume, and if lines are already coated, we recommend jetting.

Backflow Testing and Devices

Commercial buildings often have backflow preventers on the fire line, irrigation, boilers, carbonated drink systems or kitchen equipment. These devices protect the drinking water from contamination, and many need to be tested on a regular schedule by a qualified tester.

We test, repair and replace backflow assemblies, and we help you keep paperwork in order. A device that fails a test may need new seals, springs or a full replacement. We also install new ones during build-outs through plumbing installation.

Commercial Leak Detection

We find leaks in commercial spaces with the same tools we use at homes, including acoustic listening, thermal imaging, pressure testing and moisture mapping. What we can accomplish depends on scope and access. A leak above a locked suite, inside a shared wall or under a slab can limit what is possible until the area is opened. Our leak detection page explains the methods.

Licensing, Permits and the Sanitary District

The Village states that all contractors doing work in Park Forest must be Park Forest licensed. A permit is required whether the owner or a contractor does the work, and permits are dropped off or mailed to the Building Department at 350 Victory Drive.

Some commercial build-outs also need an approved application from the Thorn Creek Basin Sanitary District. If your project adds fixtures, a kitchen or a change of use, ask us early so the approvals do not hold up your opening date. Illinois also requires plumbers to be state licensed.
