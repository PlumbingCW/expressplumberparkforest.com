Once a tank splits, repair is off the table, and every minute it runs adds to the mess. Our line is open 24/7 at (855) 575-2890. Before we get there, shut the cold water valve above the heater, turn off the power or gas control, and keep a towel or wet vac close to the base.

Your tank has failed and water is on the floor

Stop the water first, because the tank will keep refilling from the street supply until you do. We book same-day appointments when the schedule allows, including after-hours calls. Whatever is already wet, getting the supply off is what limits the damage.

  • Close the cold supply valve on top of the heater. If it will not turn, shut the main water valve for the house.
  • Switch off the breaker for an electric unit or turn a gas control to off. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • Do not touch wet cords, outlets or a panel while standing in water.
  • Take photos of the leak and the tank label for your insurer.

Signs the tank is finished

Most storage tanks give warnings long before they fail. The inner steel tank corrodes once the anode rod is spent, and the corrosion eventually punches through. The clues below are the ones we see most often.

Age past ten years or so

Check the date code on the label. A tank that old is living on borrowed time, even if it still heats.

Rust-colored hot water

If only the hot side runs brown, the tank is likely corroding inside. If both sides run brown, the problem may be the piping.

Water at the base

A wet floor under a dry top means the inner tank is leaking. No fitting repair fixes that.

Repeated repairs

Two or three service calls in a short span usually cost more than the age of the tank justifies.

Rumbling and popping

Noise from the tank means sediment is baking at the bottom and stressing the steel.

Picking a size that fits your household

Tank size is about how much hot water you need at the busiest hour, not how many bathrooms you have. We look at the number of people, the largest tub, any dishwasher or washer that draws hot water, and the first-hour rating on the label. A tank that is too small runs out in the shower, and one that is too large wastes energy keeping extra gallons warm.

A household that has grown since the last heater was installed often needs a step up. One that has shrunk can usually go down. If you are considering on-demand heat instead, our tankless water heater page covers sizing by flow rate.

Gas, electric or heat pump: choosing the fuel

Fuel options at a glance
OptionWhat it needsGood to know
Gas tankGas line, combustion air, a vent to the outdoorsRecovers hot water quickly. Venting and gas piping must meet code.
Electric tankA dedicated circuit sized to the unitNo flue to maintain. Slower recovery than gas.
Heat pump (hybrid) tankA dedicated circuit, open air space, a condensate drainUses less electricity but is taller and needs room.

The easiest swap is the same fuel in the same spot. Switching fuel is possible but adds work such as a new circuit, a new gas line or a new vent, and we say so before you decide. If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Venting, expansion tank and the pieces around the heater

A new heater is more than a tank. A gas unit needs a vent pitched and sealed correctly so exhaust leaves the house. Both fuel types need new flex connectors, a relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, and often a drain pan.

An expansion tank matters in a closed system, meaning one with a check valve or pressure reducing valve at the meter. Heated water expands, and without somewhere to go the pressure climbs and stresses fittings. We check the house pressure and add or replace the expansion tank when it makes sense. The Illinois State Plumbing Code, Section 890, governs this work.

Permits in Park Forest

The Village of Park Forest lists water heater replacement among the permit items handled by its Building Department at 350 Victory Drive. All contractors working in the Village must be Park Forest licensed. A homeowner pulling a permit alone submits a waiver, and if a contractor pulls it, the homeowner signs the proposal or application.

Permits are not taken by email or online, so they are dropped off or mailed. We walk you through what the Village expects and which steps are yours. Skipping the permit can complicate a home sale or an insurance claim later.

A heat pump water heater, in one paragraph

A heat pump water heater pulls warmth from the surrounding air to heat the tank, so it uses much less electricity than a standard electric tank. It needs open space, a condensate drain and a spot that stays reasonably warm, and it cools the room a little as it runs. It is worth a look when you are already on electric and have a basement or utility room with room to spare.

What replacement day looks like

Shut down and drain

We turn off power or gas, close the supply and drain the old tank.

Remove and prepare

The old unit goes out, and we inspect the vent, gas line, circuit and shutoff valves for problems.

Set and connect

We place the new heater, connect water, new flex lines, relief valve piping and the expansion tank if needed.

Fire up and test

We fill, purge air, restore power or gas, check combustion or electrical draw, and confirm temperature and shutoff.

Walk through

You see the controls, the relief valve and the drain, and we haul away the old tank.

What affects the cost of a water heater replacement

Tank size and efficiency

Larger tanks and higher-efficiency models cost more than basic units.

Fuel and venting changes

Swapping the same fuel in place is simpler than moving the heater or changing the vent.

Code upgrades

Expansion tanks, drain pans, new shutoffs and corrected relief piping may be needed.

Permit and inspection

Replacement is a permit item with the Village, and that adds steps.

Access and removal

Tight spaces, stairs and haul-away add time.

Homeowners insurance may help with water damage from a sudden tank failure but usually not with the replacement of a worn-out heater, so check your policy. We explain permit steps before work begins. Call us and we will talk through options for your home.
