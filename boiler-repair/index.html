Older homes in cold-climate Illinois are often heated by hot-water or steam boilers, and they tend to quit when demand is highest. Cold-weather no-heat calls are handled by calling our 24/7 line at (855) 575-2890. We also work on boilers by appointment for noises, pressure drops and annual tune-ups.

Boiler quit on a cold night

Start with the simple checks. Make sure the thermostat is calling for heat, the switch at the boiler is on, and the gas valve is open. Look at the pressure gauge and the display for a code.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. If a pipe has frozen or a radiator valve is leaking, shut the water feed and call us. We cannot see inside a wall without opening it, so we start with what we can measure.

Hot-water and steam boilers are not the same animal

How the two systems differ
PointHot-water boilerSteam boiler
What moves heatHeated water pumped through pipes to radiators or baseboardSteam rises to radiators and condenses back to water
Working pressureLow pressure with an expansion tankVery low pressure, with a sight glass showing the water level
Common problemsAir in the system, failed circulator, low pressure, bad expansion tankLow water cutoff, stuck air vents, banging pipes, dirty water
Routine careBleeding radiators, checking pressureDraining sediment, checking water level

Know which one you have before you call. A sight glass with a water line suggests steam, and a round pressure gauge with a circulator pump suggests hot water. If you are unsure, just tell us what you see.

Symptoms and what they usually point to

No heat at all

Check power, thermostat and fuel first. Then look at a tripped limit, a failed igniter, a dead circulator or a low water cutoff.

Banging or kettling

Banging in steam can be water trapped in a pipe that does not pitch correctly. Kettling in a hot-water boiler is often scale or sludge on the heat exchanger.

Low pressure

A hot-water boiler that keeps losing pressure has a leak somewhere or a failed expansion tank.

Relief valve dripping

Over-pressure from a bad expansion tank, an overfilled system or a worn valve. Do not cap it.

Cold radiators upstairs

Air trapped in the system or an unbalanced loop.

Expansion tank, pressure and relief valve

A hot-water boiler is a closed loop, and heated water expands. The expansion tank holds a cushion of air that absorbs that growth. When the diaphragm fails, pressure spikes every cycle and the relief valve starts to drip.

We check the cold fill pressure, the operating pressure and the tank's air charge. Replacing a relief valve alone without checking the expansion tank is a fix that often does not last. We also look at the fill valve for a slow leak.

Bleeding radiators and clearing air

Turn the system off

Let the circulator stop and the radiators cool.

Find the bleed valve

It is usually at the top corner of the radiator, with a small square or slotted stem.

Open slowly

Hold a rag below the valve and open it until you hear air hiss, then close it when water appears.

Check the pressure

Add water at the feed if the gauge dropped, then restart and confirm every radiator warms.

If the same radiator keeps filling with air, there is a leak or a bad air vent somewhere. We trace it rather than keep bleeding. A hidden leak can also be a job for leak detection.

Annual service pays for itself

A yearly visit before the heating season catches worn parts early. We clean the burner, test the safeties, check the vent and flue, look at the pressure and expansion tank, and test the circulator. For steam, we test the low water cutoff and drain sediment.

Boilers that burn gas or oil should also have a working carbon monoxide alarm nearby. A boiler that soots up, smells or backdrafts needs attention right away.

What affects the cost of boiler repair

Type and age

Steam, hot water, gas and oil systems each have different parts.

The failed part

A thermostat or air vent is a different job from a circulator, control board or heat exchanger.

Access

Basement layout and pipe condition change the time.

Parts availability

Older boilers may need parts that take longer to source.

After-hours calls

Cold-weather no-heat visits at night are a different scheduling situation from a daytime appointment.
