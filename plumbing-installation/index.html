A good installation looks simple from the outside, because the hard part is behind the wall. Supply lines, drains, vents and shutoffs all have to line up, and the Illinois State Plumbing Code decides how. Call (855) 575-2890 and tell us what you are adding or replacing.

Fixtures We Set and Replace

We install toilets, bathroom and kitchen faucets, vanities, utility sinks, hose bibs and laundry connections. Every fixture gets new supply lines and a shutoff valve where one is missing, because an old stop valve that will not close turns the next repair into a bigger job.

  • Toilets, including new wax rings, closet bolts and a check that the flange sits level with the floor
  • Kitchen and bath faucets with braided stainless supply lines
  • Sink drains, P-traps and the vent connection behind the wall
  • Washing machine boxes with a drain standpipe at the right height
  • Outdoor hose bibs, ideally frost-proof, so winter does not split the pipe

If a fixture only needs to be swapped, we keep the job tight. For a leaking tap or a runny toilet, our faucet repair and toilet repair and installation pages explain what is repair and what is replacement.

Sinks, Tubs and Showers

A sink swap is a few hours. A tub or shower is another matter, because the drain, the valve and the pan must all work together. We check that the drain connects to a properly vented trap, that the shower valve is set to the right depth for the wall finish, and that the pan or tub holds water before tile goes on.

Many leaks that appear later start from skipped testing at this stage. We fill a shower pan and watch it before the wall is closed. If your walls have already shown stains, start with leak detection to find out whether the old drain is the source.

Rough-In for New Construction and Remodels

Rough-in is the pipework installed before drywall and flooring. We set drains at the correct slope, run vents to the roof or an approved air admittance device where allowed, and place supply stub-outs where your fixtures will land. After rough-in, the Village inspection happens before anything is covered.

In a remodel, the challenge is working around what is already there. Moving a toilet or a kitchen sink often means moving the drain, which is easy in a basement ceiling and harder over a slab. We measure first, then explain which layout changes are practical and which cost more than they are worth. If old pipes are in the way, we may suggest repiping while the walls are open.

Low Water Pressure and How We Trace It

Low pressure is one of the most common reasons people phone a plumber. The cause may be at one faucet, in one branch of piping, or in the whole house, so the first question is where the problem shows up. We test pressure with a gauge at an outdoor spigot, then compare it to what you get at fixtures.

Reading the pattern of low water pressure
What you noticeLikely causeWhat we check
One faucet is weakClogged aerator, worn cartridge or a partly closed stop valveRemove and clean the aerator, test the valve
Hot side only is weakSediment in the water heater or a restricted hot lineCheck the heater and hot-side shutoff
Whole house is weakPartly closed main, failing pressure reducing valve, rusted lines or a service line leakGauge test at the hose bib, check the valve and the meter
Pressure drops when several fixtures runUndersized or corroded supply pipeCompare static pressure with pressure under flow
Pressure too high, with banging pipesPressure reducing valve failing or thermal expansionGauge reading and expansion tank check

Normal household pressure typically sits between about 40 and 80 psi. Much lower than that suggests a restriction. Much higher strains fixtures and the water heater. If the whole house is weak and the meter runs with everything off, there may be a buried leak, which we trace through leak detection.

Backflow Devices and Testing

Backflow is when water moves backward into the supply, pulling in contaminated water from a hose, a sprinkler, a boiler or a commercial process. The device that stops it is a backflow preventer, usually a double check assembly, a reduced pressure zone assembly, or a vacuum breaker, depending on the hazard.

We can install and replace these devices, and testing is part of keeping them reliable. Many are tested on a schedule, and the Illinois State Plumbing Code governs where each type is needed. Irrigation systems and boilers are typical places for them. For businesses with higher-risk operations, see our commercial plumbing page. Call and we will explain what your site calls for.

Permits and Inspections in Park Forest

The Village Building Department at 350 Victory Drive states that all contractors doing work in Park Forest must be Park Forest licensed. A permit is required for homeowner and contractor work alike. A homeowner doing the work submits a waiver form, and if a contractor pulls the permit, the homeowner signs the proposal or application.

Water heater replacement is listed under traditional permits, and sewer repair permits cover installing clean-outs. Permits are dropped off or mailed, not taken by email or online. Illinois also requires plumbers to be state licensed, and you can verify a license on the IDPH tool.

What affects the cost of plumbing installation

Fixture choice

A basic faucet and a custom walk-in shower differ widely in parts and labor.

Wall and floor access

A basement ceiling is quick to reach, while a finished wall or slab takes cutting and patching.

Moving versus replacing

Putting a fixture in the same spot is simple, and relocating a drain or vent adds pipe and time.

Condition of existing pipes

Old shutoffs, corroded supply lines or an undersized drain can add work.

Permits and inspection

Most installations need a permit, and inspections shape the schedule.
