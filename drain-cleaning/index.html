A drain that is backing up right now

What to do while you wait for us

Water that rises in one fixture when another one drains usually means the blockage sits further down the line. Treat that as more than a slow sink.

  • Stop using sinks, showers, the dishwasher and the washing machine.
  • Skip chemical drain opener in a drain that is already full of standing water. It can splash back and it makes the job harder for the crew.
  • Do not flush toilets if water is coming up in a tub or a floor drain.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from low spots and floor drains.
  • If sewage reaches an outlet or an appliance, stay clear and leave the power off in that area.

How we clear a clogged drain

Most clogs are cleared with a drain cable, often called a snake. A motor spins a steel cable with a cutting head through the pipe, and the head either breaks the clog apart or hooks it so we can pull it back out. Small hand augers handle sinks and tubs, while a drum machine reaches farther into a branch line.

We always run water afterward to check the flow, not just the feel of the cable. Cabling opens a hole through a blockage. It does not scrape the full pipe wall, so grease or scale can leave a narrow channel that clogs again in a few weeks. When that pattern shows up, we explain it and talk through hydro jetting as the next step.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen drains clog differently from the rest of the house. Cooking grease cools inside the trap and the horizontal run behind the wall, where it hardens into a waxy lining that catches coffee grounds, rice and food scraps. A double basin makes it worse because both bowls feed one trap arm.

If the water backs up into the second basin, the blockage is past the tee, not in the strainer. We pull the trap, cable the branch and check the dishwasher drain connection, since that hose often shares the same line. A sink with a disposal needs a look at the disposal outlet too, and garbage disposal repair is a separate job if the unit itself is jammed or leaking.

Your best habit is simple. Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel and bin it, because the Village of Park Forest follows Metropolitan Water Reclamation District guidance that fats, oils, wipes and paper towels do not belong in the drain or the toilet. That same grease is what builds up in older branch lines, so it protects your pipes as well as the sewer system.

Shower and tub drains

Hair and soap scum cause nearly every slow shower. They wind around the stopper linkage or the pop-up assembly right under the drain opening, and further down they bind into a mat inside the trap. Pulling the stopper and clearing it by hand fixes many of these without a machine.

When the tub fills with water around your ankles and slowly empties, the clog is usually in the trap or the first stretch of pipe. If the tub, a sink and a toilet all act up together, the problem sits in a shared branch or the main drain, and we treat it that way from the start.

A drain line that leaks

Sometimes the problem is not a clog but a leak in the drain line itself. A wet cabinet floor under a sink, a stain on the ceiling below a bathroom or a musty smell near the tub can mean a loose slip joint, a worn trap or a cracked section of waste pipe. Drain leaks only show up when water is running, so they can hide for months.

We cannot see inside a wall without opening it, so we start with the visible joints, run water and watch. If the source is hidden, leak detection narrows down the spot before anyone cuts drywall. Plastic and cast iron drain lines are repaired differently, and we will tell you which one you have.

When a clog is really a main line problem

A single slow fixture is a branch problem. Several fixtures at once, a toilet that gurgles when the washer drains or a floor drain that bubbles all point toward the main line. Cabling one sink will not fix that, and it can waste your evening.

What older plumbing and Park Forest weather mean for your drains

Park Forest was built as a planned community after World War II, and plenty of homes here still have drain lines installed decades ago. Older cast iron and clay lines develop rough interior surfaces that snag debris, so a home that clogs twice in a year deserves a camera look instead of a third quick fix.

Heavy rain adds pressure on the system. The Village notes that urban flooding can cause sewage backups in basements, and it is replacing wastewater lines as part of the response. If water rises in a floor drain only during storms, the Village Flood Factor map for your address is a useful extra data point to bring up when we talk.

What a visit looks like

We ask what you saw

Which fixtures are affected, when it started and whether anything else backed up. These answers often point to the right line before we touch a tool.

We test and locate

We run water at each fixture and listen for gurgling to find where the blockage starts.

We clear the line

The cable or jet goes in through a cleanout, trap or fixture opening, and we keep working until water drains freely.

We explain what we found

You get the cause in plain words, whether it will likely return and whether a camera or repair is worth considering.

After hours and same-day drain calls

Drain trouble does not keep business hours. A sink that backs up on Saturday night or a tub that overflows before work on Monday is a real emergency to the household living with it. We take those calls and offer same-day visits when our schedule has room.

Please call rather than wait for a morning. A blockage that is fully stopped can push water into places it should never reach, and the longer it stands, the more cleanup you may face afterward.

What affects the cost of drain cleaning

Which line is blocked

A bathroom sink trap is a small job. A main drain or a long branch with several bends takes more equipment and more time.

Access to the drain

A cleanout makes the work quicker. If the only entry is a toilet pull or a roof vent, the setup is longer.

What is causing the clog

Hair is easy to clear. Packed grease, scale or tree roots may need heavier equipment or a second pass.

Time of day

Night and weekend calls take our crew out of normal scheduling, and that is reflected in the price.

Camera or follow-up work

If a clog keeps returning, a camera look or a repair adds to the total because it solves a different problem than the cable does.

Drain cleaning is usually not a permit job, though repairs that change the pipe itself may be. Some homeowner policies cover water damage from a sudden backup but not the cost of clearing a clog, so check your policy.
