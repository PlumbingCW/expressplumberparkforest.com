Storm season and sewer backups in Richton Park

When a heavy rain stalls over the south suburbs, sewers fill faster than they drain, and basements can pay for it. The signs are a floor drain that gurgles, a toilet that rises when the washer runs, or dark water seeping around the laundry tub.

Richton Park's wastewater is treated by the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District, while the Village maintains a lift station, 1,124 manholes and 42 miles of sewer line. That means the pipe under your street is the Village's concern. The pipe from your house to the street is yours, so sewer line cleaning usually starts there.

Winter freezes and the cold-snap calls

Freeze-thaw cycles are normal here, and pipes in exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces suffer most. Frozen pipes often split at a weak spot and do not leak until the ice melts. If you find a frozen line, shut the main off before it thaws and call, because a burst pipe repair is far smaller than a flooded finished basement.

Homes on wells and what that changes

The Village says its drinking water comes from three pumping stations drawing 400 to 800 feet underground, and mineral content can leave scale in water heaters and faucets. If your fixtures crust over or your heater rumbles, ask us about flushing the tank. Hard water shortens the life of a heating element.

Call Village Hall at 4455 Sauk Trail, 708-481-8950, if you are unsure if your street is on Village water. We check pressure at your fixtures and report what we see.

Why older storm damage still matters

An EF2 tornado damaged homes and businesses here on June 7, 2008. Houses hit by wind, debris or later repairs can carry patched pipes that were never fully inspected. If you bought a rebuilt home, a camera run on the sewer line and a pressure test on the supply are a sensible first step.

Before We Arrive in Richton Park

Stop the water first

Turn the main valve clockwise before you do anything else. In a Richton Park house on a private well, you can also switch off the well pump breaker, which stops the pressure tank from feeding the leak.

Power off, then step back

A flooded basement can hide live outlets and the furnace. Flip off breakers from a dry floor, and if the panel itself sits in water, stay out and tell us on the phone.

Lift what water can ruin

Boxes, laundry and electronics belong on shelves or upstairs. Skip trying to mop sewage yourself. Gloves and boots help, but the cleanup is easier once the backup is cleared.
