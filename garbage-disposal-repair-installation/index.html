Emergency Garbage Disposal Repair in Park Forest, IL

A disposal that hums, leaks or backs up the sink can stop a kitchen cold, and we repair, replace and install units safely.

A kitchen sink full of dirty water and a disposal that only hums is a miserable way to end a dinner. We dispatch 24/7, and there are safe things you can do right now to keep the problem from getting worse.

Turn off the disposal switch, then cut power at the breaker or unplug the unit before you touch anything. Never put a hand into the drain opening, even when the power looks off. If water is standing in the sink and the other drain line seems slow too, a blockage past the disposal may need drain cleaning rather than a new unit.

Disposal Leaking, Jammed or Backing Up the Sink

Water pouring from the bottom of the unit or the cabinet floor is the most urgent version, because the leak often sits next to the electrical connection. Turn off the power first, then stop the water.

If the sink will not drain at all, stop running the faucet and the dishwasher. We schedule same-day visits when available, and after-hours calls go to a dispatcher who can help you decide what can wait.

  • Flip the disposal switch off and shut off its breaker or unplug it.
  • Do not run the dishwasher, since it drains through the disposal.
  • Place a bucket under any leak and move cleaning supplies out of the cabinet.
  • Keep hands and tools out of the opening, and use tongs to remove visible objects.

Jams, humming and the reset button

A humming noise with no grinding means the motor is getting power but the grinding plate cannot turn. Something is stuck, such as a bone fragment, a fruit pit, a bottle cap or a spoon. Left alone, the motor overheats and trips a thermal overload.

Most disposals have a hex socket in the center of the underside. With the power off, a quarter-inch hex key worked back and forth frees the plate. The red reset button on the bottom pops out when the motor overheats, and pressing it back in restores power once the jam is cleared.

If the reset keeps tripping, the motor may be worn out, and repeated resets without finding the cause just cook the windings. An older unit with a seized motor is usually cheaper to replace than to rebuild.

Finding where a disposal leaks

Leaks fall into three spots, and where the drip comes from tells us a lot. A leak at the top, around the sink flange, means the seal or putty failed. A leak from the side, where the dishwasher hose or drain arm connects, is often just a loose clamp or a worn gasket.

A leak from the bottom of the body is different. That usually means the inner seal has failed or the housing has cracked, and the unit cannot be repaired. We run water into the sink with a dry paper towel under each joint to trace it, since a drip at the trap can look like a leak from the disposal.

A smell that does not go away

Odors usually come from food film stuck under the splash guard and on the chamber walls, not from the motor. A thorough scrubbing under the rubber baffle, and a clean drain line, often clears it. If the smell is like sewage and comes from several drains, the problem is in the line, and a sewer line check is the right step.

What to keep out of the disposal

A disposal is a small grinder, not a trash can. Fats, oils and grease cool and stick inside the drain line, and the Village of Park Forest asks residents not to flush or pour fats, oils, wipes or paper towels into the sewer system, following Metropolitan Water Reclamation District guidance. That advice applies to the kitchen sink as much as the toilet.

  • Grease, cooking oil and fat, even when hot, because they harden down the line.
  • Coffee grounds in quantity, which pack into a sludge in the trap.
  • Fibrous foods such as celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and form paste.
  • Bones, fruit pits and anything hard, plus non-food items like utensils.

Run cold water for several seconds before and after grinding, because cold water keeps fats solid enough to be chopped and flushed.

Replacing or adding a disposal

If the old unit is rusting, leaking or locked up, a replacement is a sensible fix. Disposals range from about one-third to one horsepower, and a bigger motor handles tougher scraps with less jamming. A family that cooks daily benefits from a mid-size unit with insulation to cut noise.

Remove the old unit

With power off, we disconnect the discharge pipe, dishwasher hose and mounting ring, and lower the old unit.

Set the new mount

A fresh sink flange and gasket go in, with the mounting ring tightened evenly so it does not leak.

Open the dishwasher knockout

New disposals have a plug in the dishwasher inlet that must be knocked out, or the dishwasher will not drain.

Connect and test

We wire or plug in the unit, reconnect the drain and run water and the dishwasher to test every joint.

Electrical safety matters here. A disposal either plugs into a cord or is hardwired to a switched circuit, and water and wiring sit close together. If the wiring looks damaged or the circuit is wrong for the unit, we stop and say so, and an electrician may need to fix it.

A single-sink installation is usually straightforward. A double sink with one disposal connects through a trap arm that we set to the right slope.

We also check the dishwasher hookup. The dishwasher drain hose should loop high under the counter or use an air gap so dirty water cannot flow back, and pairing a faucet repair with the disposal swap is easy, since both sit under the same sink. The Village adopts the Illinois State Plumbing Code and requires all contractors to be Park Forest licensed, so ask us if your job needs a permit.

What affects the cost of disposal repair or installation

Repair or replacement

A jam clear and reset is small. A cracked housing means a new unit, and the unit itself varies with motor size.

Horsepower and features

Larger motors, quieter insulation and continuous-feed or batch-feed designs all change the equipment cost.

Existing wiring and plumbing

A switch, outlet or drain arm that does not fit the new unit adds work, and some electrical fixes need a licensed electrician.

Dishwasher connection

Reconnecting a hose, adding an air gap or replacing a worn drain line changes the time on site.

Hours of the visit

Evening, weekend and holiday visits are priced differently from a scheduled daytime appointment.
