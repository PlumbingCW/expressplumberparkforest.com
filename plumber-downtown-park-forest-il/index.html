Plumbing in a mixed-use core

The Village of Park Forest describes DownTown as a pedestrian-oriented shopping and entertainment district built on the site of the original Park Forest Plaza. Main Street runs through it with brick crosswalks and wide sidewalks. Homes, theatres, an arts center and shops share the same few blocks.

That mix is why plumbing calls here vary so much. A kitchen drain in a restaurant, a restroom in a performance space and a water heater in a townhouse-style home need different tools and different questions. When you call, tell the dispatcher whether the building is a home, a shop or both.

Legacy Square and the homes near it

The Village calls Legacy Square its newest single-family neighborhood, with 63 new homes built by Bigelow Homes and a small park at the center. A regional planning case study adds that three of the homes are live/work units with 500 square feet of first-floor workspace, and five have an accessory dwelling above the garage.

Those details matter for plumbing. A live/work home can have a work sink or restroom that sees different use than a bathroom upstairs. A second dwelling over a garage may have its own fixtures and its own water heater. Tell us which part of the building has the problem so we start in the right place.

Before you call about a Downtown building

If you rent a storefront, find out who owns the pipes. Many landlords handle the main drain and water line, while the tenant handles fixtures inside the space. Knowing that before the call saves time.

For homes, locate the main shutoff. If water is spreading, close it, move valuables off the floor and call. Ask the plumber about permits before any water heater swap, because the Village lists water heater replacement among the jobs that need one.

Standards and sources

Written by the Park Forest Express Plumbing team. Plumbing work in Illinois follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890. Permit and contractor rules differ by village or city, so ask the Park Forest building office what it requires before work starts. In Park Forest, the Building Department says contractors must be Park Forest licensed and that a permit is required. You can confirm any plumber's license on the Illinois Department of Public Health license verification tool.
