Water is moving where it should not? Do these things first

Most plumbing damage grows by the minute, so the first five minutes matter more than anything we can say on this page. Find the main shutoff valve, which usually sits where the water line enters the house, often near the water meter or the front basement wall, and turn it clockwise until it stops.

  • Shut off the main valve, or the fixture stop under a sink or behind a toilet if only one fixture is leaking.
  • If water is near outlets, a panel or an appliance, stay out of the water and switch off power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Smell gas? Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else. We do not handle gas lines.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain pressure, and take photos of the damage for your insurance claim.
  • Do not run the dishwasher, washer or showers during a sewer backup, because every drain in the house feeds the same blocked line.

The problems that most often send people to the phone

Our dispatch work clusters around a handful of situations. Each one has its own service page with the details, and the short version is below.

Burst or frozen pipes

Freeze and thaw cycles split pipe walls and loosen fittings. See our burst pipe repair page for what happens after the water is off.

Sewer backups

Water rising in a floor drain or a basement tub points to a blockage in the main line. A sewer line cleaning visit clears it, and a camera shows why it happened.

No hot water

A tank that stays cold may have a failed element, a bad thermostat or a worn-out tank. Start with water heater repair and move to replacement only when the tank itself has failed.

Active leaks

A wet ceiling, a damp wall or a meter that spins with every tap closed all suggest a hidden leak. Leak detection finds the source before anyone cuts drywall.

Overflowing toilets

One toilet that overflows once is usually a local clog. Repeated overflows or gurgling in other drains is a line problem, covered under toilet repair and installation and drain work.

Failed sump pumps

A pump that hums but never moves water, or one that sits silent in a full pit, needs attention before the next storm. See sump pump installation and repair.

Park Forest water and what it does to your plumbing

The Village of Park Forest supplies drinking water from six wells about 300 feet deep, drawn from a dolomite limestone aquifer. The Village softens that groundwater with a lime and soda ash process, filters it, and adds chlorine, fluoride and orthophosphate for corrosion control. Because the water is already treated before it reaches your meter, scale tends to be less of a driver for tank failures here, though sediment and age still wear water heaters down.

What this means for you is practical. If your hot water runs out sooner than it used to, or the tank rumbles when it heats, call about water heater replacement or a tank flush rather than assuming the water is at fault. For test summaries, the Village Public Works office at 708-503-7702 can point you to the published results.

Village rules every homeowner should know about

The Park Forest Building Department at 350 Victory Drive states that all contractors doing work in the Village must be Park Forest licensed. A permit is required if you do the work yourself or if you hire a contractor, and water heater replacement is on the Village's list of permitted work. The Village follows the Illinois State Plumbing Code, Section 890.

Before you hire anyone, including us, ask who pulls the permit and sign the proposal that goes with it. Permits are dropped off or mailed rather than submitted online, so plan for that when a water heater installation or a sewer line repair is on the schedule. State licensing is separate, and you can check any plumber yourself on the Illinois Department of Public Health license lookup, as explained on our about page.

Getting around the village on a service call

Park Forest sits between US 30 on the north, Western Avenue on the east and Central Park Avenue on the west, with Sauk Trail running across it. Dispatch looks at those corridors when sending a technician, and it helps if you tell the dispatcher which side of the village you are on.

If your home is near Downtown Park Forest and Park Forest Plaza, the older core of the planned community, expect to hear questions about when the plumbing was last updated, because older drain lines are more likely to show root intrusion or settled joints. On the Eastgate side, east of Western Avenue, the questions are the same, and a camera inspection through sewer inspection answers them quickly.

What we can and cannot do

We are a plumbing dispatch operation, so some things sit outside our work. We do not repair gas lines, so a gas smell means you leave and call the gas utility or 911. We cannot see inside a wall without opening it, which is why we start with moisture tracing and a camera before cutting anything. We also cannot give a firm price over the phone for a problem nobody has seen yet.

Some jobs need a permit, an inspection or a second visit for parts. If a repair would only delay the inevitable, such as a corroded supply line that has already failed twice, we will tell you that and talk about repiping instead.
